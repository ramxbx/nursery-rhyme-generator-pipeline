A little snail with silver shell,
Came creeping from the garden well.
The rain had washed the world so clean,
The leaves were bright and glossy green.

He climbed a stone, he climbed a leaf,
He took his time, he had no grief.
For snails are never in a rush,
They travel slow through fern and brush.

A puddle shone like polished glass,
Reflecting all the bending grass.
He watched a beetle sail across,
Upon a little boat of moss.

And when the sun came peeping through,
And painted rainbows in the dew,
He curled inside his silver door,
And slept until it rained some more.
